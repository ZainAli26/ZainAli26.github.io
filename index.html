About

The gap between a model that works in a notebook and one that works on the device in someone's hand.

That gap is where most of my career has been spent. At PackageX it's document understanding for logistics, running on the device rather than in the cloud. Before that, at Veeve, it was a real-time vision pipeline on a shopping trolley in live grocery stores.

The through-line is compute-constrained inference: quantisation and what it costs in accuracy, LoRA instead of full fine-tunes, gating expensive models so they only run when they're needed, and a thesis deliberately capped at a single 12 GB GPU. I'd rather have a measured number than a claimed one.

Education

MS Artificial Intelligence, NUTech

Thesis completed · “Compute-Constrained Benchmarking of Post-Treatment Glioma Segmentation: An Evaluation-Centric Study on BraTS 2024 GLI” · paper under peer review

Post-treatment glioma segmentation matters for response assessment but is under-studied next to pre-operative BraTS, and state of the art assumes 24–40 GB GPUs and heavy ensembling. The thesis instead trains one nnU-Net plan inside an 11 GB memory target (fitting a 12 GB consumer GPU) across seven pre-registered single-variable arms — synthetic registration-based data, a T1c−T1n subtraction channel, focal loss, a shrunk 31.4M-parameter encoder, a SegResNetDS swap — on 1,621 training cases and 153 strictly held-out test cases from BraTS 2024 GLI.

Under pre-registered ±0.01 materiality, no modeling intervention significantly improves on baseline: the whole modeling spread is 0.011 Dice. Scoring the identical held-out predictions under five evaluation conventions shifts Dice by 0.227 — twenty times the modeling effect. Within a consumer compute budget, evaluation-pipeline choices dominate modeling choices, and reporting practice should reflect that.
